Your ClawBox answers where you already are. **Settings → Channels** sets up each one, on
both [editions](/editions/overview), and each panel shows what the channel is doing right
now rather than only whether it saved.

## Telegram

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a bot">
    Open Telegram, search **@BotFather**, send `/newbot`, and copy the token it gives you.
    The panel shows a QR code that opens BotFather on your phone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste the token">
    **Settings → Channels → Telegram**. The box checks the token with Telegram and waits
    for the channel to come up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve who may talk to it">
    A new sender messages the bot; a pairing request appears on the ClawBox screen for you
    to approve. Nobody gets an answer until you do.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Group behaviour depends on your bot's privacy settings. Start with one-to-one pairing,
then test a group deliberately.

## Discord

You need only the **bot token**. The box reads your application id and your servers from
Discord itself and builds the invite link for you.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the bot">
    In the Discord Developer Portal, create an application, add a bot and copy its token.
    On the same tab turn **Public Bot** off — otherwise anyone can add your bot to their
    server.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable the Message Content intent">
    Without it the bot appears online and never sees a message. The panel says so
    explicitly if it is missing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste the token">
    **Settings → Channels → Discord**. There is no Application ID field to fill in.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the bot to a server">
    Use the button in the panel, pick your server in Discord and authorize. The panel
    finishes the setup by itself once the bot has joined, and gives that server's owner
    access. Opening the link again later sets up a second server the same way.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose who may talk to it">
    **Who may talk to the assistant** lists the members; only the people you tick get an
    answer. The server owner is selected by default.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The status card names the exact state — *Connected and listening*, *Message Content intent
is off*, *Connected, but nobody is allowed in*, *Not connected* — so a silent bot has a
cause you can act on.

The invite the box builds grants: view channels, send messages, read history, attach
files, embed links, add reactions.

## Slack

Slack connects in **Socket Mode**: the box opens the connection to Slack itself, so it
needs no public address, port forwarding or tunnel. You need a Slack workspace where you
may install apps.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the app from the manifest">
    **Settings → Channels → Slack** shows an app manifest made for your edition. Copy it,
    open [api.slack.com/apps](https://api.slack.com/apps), choose **Create New App → From a
    manifest**, pick your workspace and paste it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate the app-level token">
    In the new app, **Basic Information → App-Level Tokens → Generate Token and Scopes**:
    add the `connections:write` scope and copy the token (`xapp-…`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Install the app and copy the bot token">
    **Install App → Install to Workspace**, then copy the **Bot User OAuth Token**
    (`xoxb-…`).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste both tokens">
    Back in **Settings → Channels → Slack**, paste both and press **Connect**. The box
    checks each token with Slack before saving and tells you which one is wrong, then waits
    for the channel to come up.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve who may talk to it">
    Message the bot directly in Slack. The first time, it replies with a pairing code;
    enter it under **Approve a new sender** in the panel. Nobody gets an answer until you
    do.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The tokens are kept out of the assistant's configuration file: on the OpenClaw edition
they are stored as environment references read only by the gateway, and on the Hermes
edition in Hermes' own environment file.

## WhatsApp

**Settings → Channels → WhatsApp** pairs the box with your WhatsApp account and shows
whether it is paired, enabled but unpaired, or actively receiving.

## Email

**Settings → Channels → Email** connects a mailbox and offers three modes, under *What the
assistant may do with this mailbox*:

| Mode | What it allows |
|---|---|
| **Send only** | The assistant can send email from this account. It never opens the mailbox. |
| **Read on demand** | It can also look at recent messages, but only when you ask it to. It never checks the mailbox on its own. |
| **Answer senders** | It watches the mailbox and replies to the people you list, without asking you first. Hermes edition only. |

<Warning>
Whether the assistant may put a message on the wire by itself is one switch: **Ask me
before sending**, on by default. While it is on, every message the assistant writes waits
as a draft in **Settings → Email** and in the chat, where you read it and choose **Send
it** or **Delete it without sending**. You can also have every waiting draft posted to a
second Telegram bot with an Approve button, so you can send it without opening the page. Turn the switch off and the assistant
sends on its own — the panel says so under the switch.

**Answer senders** is separate and is not covered by that switch: in that mode the Hermes
edition's own mail gateway watches the mailbox and replies in-thread to the addresses you
list, without asking you first.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Receiving** mail is a Hermes-edition feature. On other editions the inbound fields are
not shown rather than being offered and doing nothing; sending works on every edition.
</Note>

## Spoken replies on channels

With [Spoken replies](/guides/chat#voice) on, a voice note sent to a channel gets a spoken
answer back. This is an OpenClaw-edition feature — on the Hermes edition the box still
answers out loud in the desktop chat, and channel replies stay in text.

## Preview channels

Some channels have a guided setup that ships **switched off**, because something they
need on the box is not settled yet. Each panel says exactly what is missing. To try one,
add a line to `/home/clawbox/clawbox/.env` and restart the ClawBox service:

```bash
CLAWBOX_PREVIEW_CHANNELS=signal,msteams
```

Remove the line (and restart) to hide it again; nothing else changes.

### Signal

Signal needs **signal-cli** on the box, which ClawBox does not install yet (on the Jetson
it needs a Java runtime). With signal-cli installed:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use a separate number">
    Give the assistant its own Signal number. On your personal number it ignores the
    messages you send yourself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link the box">
    **Settings → Channels → Signal → Show QR code**, then on that phone open **Signal →
    Settings → Linked devices → Link new device** and scan it. The box finishes the setup
    by itself once the phone confirms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve who may talk to it">
    A new sender gets a pairing code from the bot; enter it under **Approve a new sender**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

On the OpenClaw edition the gateway starts and supervises signal-cli itself. The Hermes
edition connects to a signal-cli service it does not start, so until the box runs one the
panel says that the channel is saved but cannot reach Signal.

### Microsoft Teams

Teams delivers messages to the box over **public HTTPS**, and ClawBox does not open a
public address by itself yet — you provide one (a reverse proxy or tunnel) that forwards
to the box. The Teams panel shows the exact local port and path to forward to: the
gateway's own port on the OpenClaw edition, Hermes' Teams port (3978 by default) on the
Hermes edition.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the bot">
    In the Azure portal create an **Azure Bot** (single tenant). Copy the **Microsoft App
    ID** and the **Directory (tenant) ID**, then under **Certificates & secrets** create a
    client secret and copy its **Value**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point the messaging endpoint at the box">
    Set the bot's messaging endpoint to `https://<your public address>/api/messages`,
    forwarded to the port the panel shows. Add the **Microsoft Teams** channel to the bot
    and install the app in Teams.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste the three values">
    **Settings → Channels → Microsoft Teams**. The box checks them with Microsoft before
    saving, then reports the channel as *listening* — it cannot see from inside whether
    Teams reaches it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve who may talk to it">
    A new sender gets a pairing code from the bot; enter it under **Approve a new sender**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Other OpenClaw channels

OpenClaw supports more channels than ClawBox sets up for you — Matrix and Google Chat
among them. Treat those as advanced configuration: they
work where your installed OpenClaw version and its channel plugins support them, and they
have no guided setup in the ClawBox UI.

iMessage works only on a Mac, so ClawBox does not offer it; the Channels page says so.

## Troubleshooting

If a channel will not link or stops replying, start with its own status card — it names
the state — then see [Troubleshooting](/support/troubleshooting).
